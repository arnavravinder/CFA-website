Chronic Sleep Deprivation: The Long-term Impact of Poor Sleep

By Alpana Pillai

Sleep is one of the most crucial actions that can be taken to aid the body in its performance, from maintaining cognitive and emotional functions to regulation of important processes, it remains key for every individual. Despite this, many seem to lose sleep, and while on a rare occurrence it does not pose a major effect, regular deprivation is extremely dangerous.

Sleep deprivation is the outcome of not getting enough sleep, or sleep of poor quality, and when it happens over an extensive period it tends to cause both physical and emotional issues for the individual. As chronic sleep deprivation becomes increasingly prevalent, especially within the youth, it becomes crucial to understand the dangers that it poses, and what steps can be taken to prevent it.

Understanding Chronic Sleep Deprivation

Sleep deprivation can either be short term, lasting a few nights for example, or it can last weeks to years. The latter is known as chronic sleep deprivation, and it can often be divided into two distinct variables, a less than needed amount of sleep, or poor quality sleep.

A study done in 2024 by HCP shows that 31% of 68,000 adults globally don't get the necessary amount of sleep. One of the plausible reasons for this is that individuals lack awareness on how much sleep they require, hence making it imperative for the population to know the recommended hours of sleep for each age group.

Age Group Recommended Hours of Sleep
Newborns 14 - 17 hours
Infants 12 - 16 hours
Young Children 10 - 14 hours
School-aged Children 9 - 12 hours
Teenagers 8 - 10 hours
Adults 7 - 9 hours

Apart from a lack of understanding of the required amount of sleep, another major influence on loss of sleep is through modern influences. With the rise in consumption of social media, screens and caffeine, much of the youth tend to go to sleep at a much later time than suggested. Furthermore, studies (NIH) have shown that over the past two decades, there has been a rise in academic pressure, hence much of the sleep deprivation may be caused by late nights spent working or studying by students. Lastly, there are certain factors that are, for the most part, out of the individual's control, such as environmental factors or sleep disorders (insomnia, obstructive sleep apnea etc.), which make the individual’s ability to fall asleep much harder.

Ultimately, chronic sleep deprivation is much more complicated than just fatigue, and should not be viewed as a simple side effect of a busy lifestyle. Long-term loss of sleep can interfere with crucial functions and processes, making it crucial to maintain health.

The Physical and Emotional Impact of Sleep Deprivation

When it comes to the impacts of sleep deprivation, the issues spread across the physical and emotional facets. Sleep plays an important role in memory, immunity, metabolism and emotional regulation, hence a loss of it can have negative impacts on both the mind and body.

Physical Impact:

The physical impacts of sleep deprivation extend across numerous bodily functions.

  • Nervous System - The nervous system holds the function of processing information, hence, with sleep deprivation, the ability to concentrate, learn, and process information often falls.
  • Immune System - During sleep, the immune system produces antibodies, which are used to fight bacteria. A lack of sleep impairs the production of these antibodies, causing immunity and the ability to fight off diseases to reduce.
  • Cardiovascular System - Lack of sleep causes an increase in blood sugar, blood pressure and inflammation, causing a greater risk of cardiovascular diseases, such as strokes or heart attacks.
  • Digestive System - The two hormones, leptin and ghrelin are responsible in conveying the feeling of hunger and fullness, without enough sleep, the production of ghrelin increases, causing an appetite stimulant, which can distort metabolism and lead to obesity.

Emotional Impact:

While sleep deprivation does have an immense impact on physical health, it also poses immense dangers to mental health.

  • Emotional Instability - Studies have shown that sleep deprivation has been linked with increased levels of anxious behavior, aggression levels, mood fluctuations and depression.
  • Behavioural and Psychological Changes - Sleep deprivation has also been linked with mental changes in individuals, such as reduced attention spans, greater irritability, impaired emotional regulation, and poorer interpersonal connections, which can greatly cause issues in marriages, families and friendships.

The effects that sleep deprivation poses extend far beyond what is assumed. Through its influence on cognitions, physical capability and emotional wellbeing, sleep deprivation can greatly impact quality of life and long-term health. This makes addressing persistent sleep deprivation extremely crucial before the consequences become more severe.

How to Manage Sleep Deprivation

Chronic sleep deprivation can occur from a mix of emotional, medical and environmental factors. Late night work and schedules often encourage individuals to prioritize work over sleep, while technology can further delay it. Although, insufficient sleep is not always a choice, as stress, medical conditions and the environment can also prevent individuals from obtaining adequate rest.

Regardless of the reason for sleep deprivation, it is vital that individuals understand the methods they can use to help prevent chronic deprivation. Firstly, creating a comfortable nighttime environment, with a routine, can be immensely helpful. Actions such as meditation or a warm bath, for example, bring the mind peace and hence allow the body to fall asleep appropriately. Consistency with these actions can create a routine which helps individuals get a good night's sleep on a normal basis. Another method that aids in falling asleep is limitation. Limiting the usage of screens, caffeine, harsh lighting and rigorous physical activities right before sleeping can all help individuals fall asleep at a more appropriate time, and ensure that the sleep is of suitable quality.

Ultimately, improving sleep requires both recognition and consistency from the individual's part. Establishing healthy sleep habits can help reduce preventable sleep loss. When it comes to sleep deprivation through disorders or health issues, seeking aid from a professional is the best method to help get quality sleep.

Sleep is not simply a period of inactivity, but a biological process that helps recovery of the mind and body. Chronic sleep deprivation impacts numerous aspects of an individual’s life, making it crucial to take the necessary steps to reduce its effect. Though occasional sleep loss may be unavoidable, consistently getting inadequate sleep should not be normalized under any circumstances.
