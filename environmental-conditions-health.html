Health In India: The Hidden Dangers of Environmental Conditions

By Alpana Pillai

In recent years, India has been releasing around 4 billion tons of greenhouse gases, this makes the country the third biggest contributor to global greenhouse gas emissions. India’s harsh environmental conditions pose multiple issues for the country, but in regards to health, studies have shown that air pollution alone is responsible for 1.5 million deaths annually.

India’s environment continues to deteriorate at an extreme rate, with the air quality around the country being exceedingly above the safe standard. While air pollution remains as the most severe and dangerous form of pollution in India, numerous other kinds still plague Indians’ health.

India’s Environmental Issues:

India faces many severe environmental issues, the three most severe are air pollution, water pollution, and waste management.

Air Pollution:
Reports have shown that as of 2025, India ranked as the 6th most polluted country and has consistently ranked among the most polluted countries in the world.

Daily, Indians suffer from extreme air pollution and declines in AQI (Air Quality Index) as a country populated by more than 1.4 billion people generates greenhouse gas emissions by the gigaton. Fortunately, India is making rapid developments in terms of clean energy usage to hopefully minimize air pollution in the upcoming years. Additionally, while most countries globally have a large divide between the air pollution levels in rural and urban areas, India maintains almost the same pollution levels. Emissions in urban areas are typically composed of vehicles, with around 20-30% of pollution being attributed to them. On the other hand, in rural India, household pollution that mainly stems from burning of biomass is the largest factor for the worsening air quality.

Water Pollution:
As of 2025, pollution impacts nearly 70% of water bodies in India.

While air pollution tends to be the largest contributor to India’s damaged environment, water pollution still exists as a large-scale crisis. India’s large population leads to an immense amount of domestic waste, 80% of which goes untreated and hence contributes to waste buildup in rivers, lakes and other bodies of water.

Waste Management:
Articles from 2024 have shown that 5% of waste generated in India is recycled, 18% is composted and the remaining 77% end up in landfills.

The poor waste management in India can be attributed to many reasons, such as the poor infrastructure making waste treatment difficult or a deficiency in finances that limits the population from effectively taking care of waste. The waste management in India has consistently been an issue, with a growing population and the rapid urbanization the country generates around 160,000-170,000 tons of solid waste on a daily basis.

These three environmental issues are leading causes for many of the complications India faces, one of the most prominent ones being health.

The Impact on Health:

Harsh environmental conditions can lead to the development of life-threatening diseases. India, which has one of the world's lowest air quality scores, makes up around an extremely significant percentage of all global deaths attributed to air pollution. Millions of people in India suffer from numerous diseases that stem from the severe levels of pollution, waste management or climate change as a whole. The decline in air and water quality have led to an increased amount of illness and deaths.

Water pollution has led to an increase in waterborne diseases that are consistently reducing the sanitation of drinking water. Between February 2025 and January 2026, 5,500 people fell sick and 34 died due to contamination in tap water. As water pollution levels continuously rise, issues with unsafe water have grown and transitioned from a monsoon-only to a year-around problem. Furthermore, air pollution also remains a dire safety hazard for Indians. In 2023, air pollution caused around 2 million deaths. Most illnesses attributed to air pollution include lung diseases, respiratory infections, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and strokes among others. Poor waste management in India is often a middle ground, linking both water and air pollution. The burning of waste creates air pollution and similarly, poor disposal of waste creates water contamination. Waste management additionally also contributes to malaria and dengue (caused from the accumulation of garbage).

India’s large population and poor management of the environment creates a breeding ground for diseases and severe health issues. Yet, India’s government is implementing plans that will help them achieve their goals of eliminating the severity of these hazards. Hence, in the future it is likely that the gravity of pollution on public health will gradually reduce and help Indians lead a healthier and more prosperous life.
